An Oregon traffic ticket is usually a violation, not a crime. The penalty is a fine, not jail. But the fine is only part of the cost. Paying the ticket is a plea of no contest, the violation goes on your driving record, and a string of convictions can lead DMV to restrict or suspend your license. A few charges, such as reckless driving and some driving-while-suspended cases, are crimes. If your license, your job or your insurance rates depend on a clean record, learn your options before the appearance date printed on the citation.

## Is a traffic ticket a crime in Oregon?

Most are not. Oregon law defines a violation as an offense punished by a fine but not by jail, and a violation conviction does not carry the legal disadvantages of a criminal conviction ([ORS 153.008](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_153.008)). Traffic violations come in four classes, A to D ([ORS 153.012](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.012%20Violation%20categories.)). Each class has a presumptive fine, which is the amount usually written on the ticket ([ORS 153.019](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.019%20Presumptive%20fines%3B%20generally.)), and a maximum fine a judge can impose ([ORS 153.018](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.018%20Maximum%20fines.)).

| Class | Common examples                                                                                                                                                             | Presumptive fine | Maximum fine |
| ----- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------ |
| A     | Speeding more than 30 mph over (more than 20 over where the limit is 65 or higher); careless driving that contributes to a crash; driving while suspended (violation level) | $440             | $2,000       |
| B     | Speeding 21 to 30 mph over (11 to 20 over where the limit is 65 or higher); careless driving                                                                                | $265             | $1,000       |
| C     | Speeding 11 to 20 mph over (up to 10 over where the limit is 65 or higher)                                                                                                  | $165             | $500         |
| D     | Speeding 1 to 10 mph over where the limit is under 65                                                                                                                       | $115             | $250         |

The speed classes come from [ORS 811.109](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.109), careless driving from [ORS 811.135](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.135) and violation-level driving while suspended from [ORS 811.175](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.175). Some driving charges are crimes instead. Reckless driving is a Class A misdemeanor ([ORS 811.140](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.140)), and driving while suspended becomes a crime when the suspension came from certain serious offenses ([ORS 811.182](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.182)). For more detail on each class, read our post on [Oregon traffic violation classes and fines](/blog/oregon-traffic-violation-classes-fines/).

## What happens if you just pay the ticket?

Paying the fine before your appearance date is a no-contest plea. It waives your trial and lets the court enter a judgment against you ([ORS 153.061](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.061%20Appearance%20by%20defendant.)). The Oregon Judicial Department's [traffic options page](https://www.courts.oregon.gov/services/online/Pages/Traffic-Options.aspx) says it plainly: only a not-guilty verdict at trial or a dismissal by a judge keeps a violation off your driving record.

That record matters:

- **Driver Improvement Program.** Under Oregon DMV's [Driver Improvement Program](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/driverid/suspreasons.aspx), a driver 18 or older with three convictions or preventable crashes in 24 months gets a 30-day restriction (no driving from midnight to 5 am). Five in 24 months brings a 30-day suspension.
- **Habitual offender.** Twenty or more convictions for listed traffic offenses in five years means DMV revokes your license for five years ([ORS 809.600](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.600)).
- **Insurance.** Premiums may rise when convictions reach your record.

## What happens if you ignore the ticket?

> **Deadline:** The citation lists a date and time to appear. If you miss it, the court can notify DMV to begin suspending your driving privileges ([ORS 809.220](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.220)). Knowingly failing to appear on a violation citation is also a crime, a Class A misdemeanor ([ORS 153.992](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_153.992)).

## How do you fight a traffic ticket in Oregon?

You plead not guilty and ask for a trial, in person or in writing, before the date and time on the citation. Some courts also allow trial by affidavit, where you and the officer submit written statements instead of testifying ([Oregon Judicial Department](https://www.courts.oregon.gov/services/online/Pages/Traffic-Options.aspx)). At a violation trial ([ORS 153.076](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_153.076)):

- A judge decides the case. There is no jury.
- The state must prove the violation by a preponderance of the evidence. That is a lower bar than the proof beyond a reasonable doubt required in criminal cases ([ORS 10.095](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_10.095)).
- Pretrial discovery rules apply, so the defense can ask for the state's evidence.
- You cannot be required to testify.
- No lawyer is appointed for you. If you want one, you hire one.

If you come without a lawyer, a prosecutor generally does not appear, and the officer who wrote the ticket may present the case ([ORS 153.083](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_153.083)). If you bring a lawyer, a district attorney or city attorney may appear for the state.

There is a trade-off. After a no-contest plea, the fine cannot exceed the presumptive fine. After a trial, the judge can impose any fine up to the legal maximum ([ORS 153.090](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_153.090)). Whether fighting makes sense depends on your record and on what a conviction would cost you. ## Which tickets need extra care?

Some tickets carry consequences well beyond the fine. Each of these has its own page:

- **Commercial drivers.** A CDL holder's ticket cannot be kept off the record through diversion, and some convictions, even in a personal car, suspend a CDL. Read about [CDL tickets for commercial drivers](/traffic-tickets/cdl-commercial-drivers/).
- **Speeding.** Driving 100 mph or more brings a fixed fine and a mandatory suspension. Read how to [fight a speeding ticket in Oregon](/traffic-tickets/speeding/).
- **Driving while suspended.** It can be a violation, a misdemeanor or a felony, depending on why you were suspended. Read about [driving while suspended in Oregon](/traffic-tickets/driving-while-suspended/).
- **Reckless or careless driving.** One is a crime with a DMV suspension; the other is a violation that grows serious after a crash. Read about [reckless and careless driving charges](/traffic-tickets/reckless-and-careless-driving/).

## What if you drive for a living?

If you drive a cab, a school bus or a truck, or you run a landscaping or construction business out of your pickup, a suspension can mean lost income. DMV offers [hardship permits](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/driverid/hardship.aspx) for some suspensions. A hardship permit can allow driving to and from work, on the job and to look for work, and self-employed drivers can qualify by documenting their business. A hardship permit covers non-commercial vehicles only. Oregon law bars any hardship permit that authorizes driving a commercial motor vehicle ([ORS 807.240](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_807.240)).

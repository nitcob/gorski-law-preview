Reckless driving in Oregon is a crime. It is a Class A misdemeanor, punishable by up to 364 days in jail and a $6,250 fine, and a conviction triggers a DMV suspension. Careless driving is not a crime. It is a traffic violation punished by a fine, though the rules get much stricter when careless driving contributes to a crash that seriously injures or kills a pedestrian, cyclist or other vulnerable road user. The difference often comes down to one word in the law: "recklessly."

## What is the difference between reckless and careless driving?

| Topic                    | Reckless driving                                                                                                                                                  | Careless driving                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Law                      | [ORS 811.140](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.140)                                                                                                     | [ORS 811.135](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.135)                                                                                                        |
| Type                     | Crime: Class A misdemeanor                                                                                                                                        | Traffic violation: Class B, or Class A if it contributes to an accident                                                                                              |
| What the state must show | Driving recklessly in a way that endangers people or property                                                                                                     | Driving in a way that endangers, or would be likely to endanger, people or property                                                                                  |
| Maximum penalty          | 364 days in jail and a $6,250 fine ([ORS 161.615](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.615); [ORS 161.635](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.635)) | $1,000 fine (Class B) or $2,000 (Class A) ([ORS 153.018](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.018%20Maximum%20fines.))           |
| Presumptive fine         | None; the court sets any fine up to the maximum                                                                                                                   | $265 (Class B) or $440 (Class A) ([ORS 153.019](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.019%20Presumptive%20fines%3B%20generally.)) |
| DMV suspension           | Yes, on conviction                                                                                                                                                | No automatic suspension; see the vulnerable-user rules below                                                                                                         |

Both laws apply on highways and on premises open to the public, which Oregon defines as any place open to the general public for driving, whether publicly or privately owned ([ORS 801.400](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_801.400)).

## What counts as reckless driving in Oregon?

A person commits reckless driving by recklessly driving a vehicle in a way that endangers the safety of people or property. "Recklessly" has a specific legal meaning: the driver was aware of a substantial and unjustifiable risk and consciously disregarded it, and doing so was a gross deviation from what a reasonable person would do ([ORS 161.085](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.085)). Careless driving has no such awareness requirement.

Oregon's law also covers intentionally breaking the traction of the rear tires, or spinning them so they leave marks on the road, in front of two or more people gathered to watch ([ORS 811.140](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.140)).

## What happens after a reckless driving conviction?

The jail and fine limits are only part of it:

- **DMV suspension.** DMV suspends your driving privileges on receiving the conviction ([ORS 809.411](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.411)). The suspension is 90 days for a first offense, one year for a second and three years for a third or later, when the offenses fall within five years ([ORS 809.428](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.428)).
- **Proof of insurance to reinstate.** DMV will not reinstate until you file proof of future responsibility, an [SR-22](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/driverid/sr22.aspx) certificate from your insurer.
- **Driving during that suspension.** For driving before January 1, 2027, driving while suspended for reckless driving is criminal DWS, a Class A misdemeanor ([ORS 811.182](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.182)). A 2026 law, [House Bill 4041](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2026R1/Measures/Overview/HB4041), takes reckless driving off that list for driving on or after January 1, 2027. From then on, it is violation DWS, a Class A traffic violation ([Oregon Laws 2026, chapter 14](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/lawsstatutes/2026orLaw0014.pdf), sections 1 and 2; [ORS 811.175](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.175)). Read more in our post on [whether driving while suspended is a crime in Oregon](/blog/driving-while-suspended-oregon-violation-or-crime/).
- **Habitual offender count.** Reckless driving is one of the offenses that count toward a five-year habitual offender revocation ([ORS 809.600](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.600)).
- **CDL impact.** Reckless driving in any vehicle is a serious traffic offense for a CDL holder, according to [Oregon DMV](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/driverid/federal_cdl_reqs.aspx). See [CDL tickets for commercial drivers](/traffic-tickets/cdl-commercial-drivers/).
- **Work zones and school zones.** In a highway work zone or a posted school zone, the minimum fine for reckless driving is 20 percent of the maximum ([ORS 811.230](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.230); [ORS 811.235](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.235)).

Oregon law also lets a prosecutor choose to treat many misdemeanors as Class A violations. DUII and eluding police are excluded; reckless driving is not ([ORS 161.566](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors161.html#:~:text=161.566%20Misdemeanor%20treated%20as%20violation%3B%20prosecuting%20attorney%E2%80%99s%20election.)). Whether that happens is the prosecutor's decision.

## When does careless driving become serious?

A careless driving Oregon ticket is a Class B violation, or Class A if it contributes to an accident ([ORS 811.135](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.135)). The Oregon courts do not offer online fine reductions for careless driving ([Traffic Options](https://www.courts.oregon.gov/services/online/Pages/Traffic-Options.aspx)).

The stakes rise when careless driving contributes to the serious physical injury or death of a vulnerable user of a public way. That includes pedestrians, highway workers, people riding an animal, and people on bicycles, motorcycles, mopeds or scooters ([ORS 801.608](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_801.608)).

If the officer notes this on the citation, you must appear in court in person ([ORS 153.061](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.061%20Appearance%20by%20defendant.)). If the court finds that the careless driving contributed to the injury or death:

- The court must order a traffic safety course and 100 to 200 hours of community service.
- The court must also order a fine of up to $12,500 and a one-year suspension, but suspend both on condition that you complete the course and the service.
- At a hearing up to a year later, the court dismisses those penalties if you completed the requirements, or imposes them (or grants more time for good cause) if you did not.

In Oregon, driving while suspended (DWS) is usually a traffic violation, not a crime. It becomes a crime when the suspension or revocation came from a serious offense listed in the statute, such as DUII, a refused or failed breath test, reckless driving (for driving before January 1, 2027) or a hit-and-run with injuries. So the first question in any DWS case is simple: why was the license suspended in the first place? The answer decides whether you face a fine, up to 364 days in jail, or, in a few situations, a felony charge.

## When is driving while suspended a violation, and when is it a crime?

Driving a motor vehicle while your privileges are suspended or revoked is "violation driving while suspended," a Class A traffic violation ([ORS 811.175](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.175)). The same law covers driving outside the limits of a hardship permit, and driving a commercial vehicle while your commercial privileges are suspended in any state. It is "criminal driving while suspended" when the suspension is one listed in [ORS 811.182](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.182).

| A reason not listed in ORS 811.182, such as a missed court date on a traffic ticket or the Driver Improvement Program                                                                                                                                                             | Class A traffic violation | $2,000 fine; presumptive fine $440 ([ORS 153.018](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.018%20Maximum%20fines.); [ORS 153.019](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.019%20Presumptive%20fines%3B%20generally.)) |

| Murder, manslaughter, criminally negligent homicide or assault involving a vehicle; aggravated vehicular homicide; aggravated DWS; or a revocation for felony DUII                                                                                                                | Class B felony            | 10 years in prison and a $250,000 fine ([ORS 161.605](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.605); [ORS 161.625](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.625))                                                                                                                  |

> **Note:** A 2026 law, [House Bill 4041](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2026R1/Measures/Overview/HB4041), takes suspensions for reckless driving, and for criminal mischief resulting from driving, off the misdemeanor list in ORS 811.182. The change becomes operative January 1, 2027, and applies only to driving on or after that date ([Oregon Laws 2026, chapter 14](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/lawsstatutes/2026orLaw0014.pdf), sections 1 and 2). From then on, driving during one of those suspensions falls under ORS 811.175 instead: a Class A traffic violation.

If the underlying suspension came from DUII, a criminal DWS conviction carries a minimum fine of $1,000 for a first conviction and $2,000 for a second or later one ([ORS 811.182](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.182)). If your charge is in the middle row, a DWS Oregon misdemeanor, it is a criminal case, and a conviction is a criminal conviction. Our post on [whether driving while suspended is a crime in Oregon](/blog/driving-while-suspended-oregon-violation-or-crime/) goes through each listed suspension in more detail.

## What else can happen after a DWS stop?

The charge is not the only consequence:

- **Impound at the stop.** An officer with probable cause may have the vehicle impounded on the spot ([ORS 809.720](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.720)).
- **Court-ordered impound.** After a DWS conviction, a court may order the vehicle impounded or immobilized for up to one year ([ORS 809.700](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.700)).
- **Forfeiture.** A vehicle may be seized for forfeiture on a criminal DWS charge if the driver has a criminal DWS conviction in the previous three years ([ORS 809.740](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.740)).
- **Habitual offender status.** Three criminal DWS, DUII, reckless driving or other listed convictions within five years lead DMV to revoke your license for five years ([ORS 809.600](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.600)).
- **Aggravated DWS.** If someone knowingly drives while suspended for a criminal offense involving a vehicle and causes serious physical injury or death, the charge is a Class C felony ([ORS 163.196](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_163.196)).

## What if you didn't know your license was suspended?

Oregon law gives two affirmative defenses ([ORS 811.180](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.180)). One is necessity: an injury or immediate threat of injury to a person or animal made it necessary to drive. The other is lack of notice: you had not received notice of the suspension and no judge had told you about it.

The notice defense is not available if:

- You refused to accept DMV's notice, including refusing to sign for certified mail.
- DMV could not deliver the notice because you had not reported a change of address.
- A judge told you at an earlier court appearance that your license was being suspended.
- You knew about the suspension by any means before the stop.
- You were given a notice of intent to suspend after a breath or blood test request.

## Why was my license suspended, and how do I check?

You can [check your driving privilege status](https://dmv2u.oregon.gov/eServices/?link=verified) through DMV2U. Common reasons for a suspension, according to [Oregon DMV](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/driverid/suspreasons.aspx), include:

- **A missed court date on a ticket.** The court notifies DMV ([ORS 809.220](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.220)). The suspension lasts until the court reports the case is cleared, or 10 years from the offense date, whichever comes first ([ORS 809.416](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.416)).
- **Too many convictions or preventable crashes** under the Driver Improvement Program.
- **Failure to pay child support.**
- **A DUII arrest or conviction.** An implied consent suspension and a DUII conviction suspension are separate.

DMV also says Oregon courts no longer request suspensions for failure to comply (unpaid fines), and the governors have remitted unpaid fines and fees in some traffic cases that led to failure-to-appear or failure-to-comply suspensions. DMV's page explains how to check. If your suspension came after a breath test, see our post on [the breath test refusal suspension and hardship permits](/blog/refusing-breath-test-oregon-suspension-hardship-permit/).

Commercial drivers face added risk. If your CDL was suspended for violations in a commercial vehicle, a conviction for driving a commercial vehicle during that suspension brings another one-year CDL suspension ([ORS 809.510](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.510)). See our page on [CDL tickets for commercial drivers](/traffic-tickets/cdl-commercial-drivers/).

Yes, you can fight a speeding ticket in Oregon. You plead not guilty and request a trial before the appearance date on the citation, and a judge decides whether the state has proved the violation. Speeding is a violation, not a crime, so for most drivers the stakes are a fine and a mark on the driving record. Two situations raise the stakes: driving 100 mph or more, which brings a mandatory suspension, and holding a CDL, where one more conviction can cost you your job.

## What are the Oregon speeding ticket classes?

Oregon grades speeding by how far over the limit you were. The scale is stricter on roads posted at 65 mph or more ([ORS 811.109](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.109)). The dollar amounts are presumptive fines ([ORS 153.019](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.019%20Presumptive%20fines%3B%20generally.)).

| Miles over the limit | Limit under 65 mph                   | Limit 65 mph or more                 |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| 1 to 10              | Class D: $115                        | Class C: $165                        |
| 11 to 20             | Class C: $165                        | Class B: $265                        |
| 21 to 30             | Class B: $265                        | Class A: $440                        |
| More than 30         | Class A: $440                        | Class A: $440                        |
| 100 mph or more      | $1,150 and a 30 to 90 day suspension | $1,150 and a 30 to 90 day suspension |

Some counties add a $5 courthouse surcharge to traffic fines ([ORS 1.188](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_1.188)).

Speeding tickets are written under one of two laws. The speed limit law makes it an offense to go faster than a posted or statutory limit ([ORS 811.111](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.111)). The basic speed rule makes it an offense to drive faster than is reasonable and prudent given traffic, the road, weather and visibility ([ORS 811.100](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.100)). Under the basic rule, a speed over a posted designated speed is prima facie evidence of a violation ([ORS 811.105](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.105)). Which law is on your citation shapes what the state has to prove.

## What is the speeding ticket fine Oregon judges can impose?

Three numbers matter:

- **Presumptive fine.** The amount usually written on the ticket.
- **Maximum fine.** $250 for Class D, $500 for Class C, $1,000 for Class B and $2,000 for Class A ([ORS 153.018](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.018%20Maximum%20fines.)).
- **Minimum fine.** $65 to $225 depending on class, though a judge may waive it, after considering your finances, when requiring it would be inconsistent with justice ([ORS 153.021](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.021%20Minimum%20fines%3B%20waiver%3B%20audit%20of%20court.)).

Fines go up in posted school zones, highway work zones and safety corridors when the officer notes it on the citation. There, the presumptive fines are $225, $325, $525 and $875 for Classes D through A ([ORS 153.020](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.020%20Presumptive%20fines%3B%20highway%20work%20zones%2C%20school%20zones%20and%20safety%20corridors.)).

How you answer the ticket also affects the fine. If you plead no contest, the fine cannot exceed the presumptive fine. After a trial, the judge can impose any fine up to the maximum ([ORS 153.090](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_153.090)). The Oregon courts offer [fine reductions](https://www.courts.oregon.gov/services/online/Pages/Traffic-Options.aspx) to some drivers who plead no contest, but some charges, such as speeding over 100 mph, are not eligible. A no-contest plea still puts the violation on your record.

## Can a speeding ticket suspend your license?

Yes, in three ways:

- **100 mph or more.** On conviction, the court must suspend your driving privileges for 30 to 90 days ([ORS 811.109](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.109)).
- **More than 30 mph over, with a recent prior.** If you also have a speeding conviction within 12 months of the new offense, the court may suspend your privileges for up to 30 days.
- **Too many convictions.** Under DMV's [Driver Improvement Program](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/driverid/suspreasons.aspx), adult drivers with five convictions or preventable crashes in 24 months face a 30-day suspension, and three bring a 30-day restriction on late-night driving.

For commercial drivers, speeding 15 mph or more over the limit in a commercial vehicle is a serious traffic offense, and two serious offenses in three years suspend a CDL. Read more on our page about [CDL tickets for commercial drivers](/traffic-tickets/cdl-commercial-drivers/).

## How do you fight a speeding ticket in Oregon?

Fighting a speeding ticket generally involves the same steps as any violation ticket:

- **The appearance date.** It is printed on the citation, and missing it can lead to a suspension ([ORS 809.220](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.220)).
- **A not-guilty plea and a request for trial,** made in person or in writing before that date. Some courts allow a trial by affidavit instead ([Oregon Judicial Department](https://www.courts.oregon.gov/services/online/Pages/Traffic-Options.aspx)).
- **Discovery.** Pretrial discovery rules apply in violation cases, so the defense can request the state's evidence ([ORS 153.076](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_153.076)).
- **The citation itself.** Which statute it cites, the posted limit and the speed alleged all matter.
- **The trial.** A judge hears the case with no jury. The state must prove the violation by a preponderance of the evidence, and you cannot be required to testify.

Without a defense lawyer, a prosecutor generally does not appear and the officer who wrote the ticket may present the case. If you bring a lawyer, a district attorney or city attorney may appear for the state ([ORS 153.083](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_153.083); [ORS 153.076](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_153.076)).

Whether a trial is worth it depends on your record, the speed alleged and what a conviction would cost you.

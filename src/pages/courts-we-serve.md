Gorski Law represents clients throughout Oregon, including eastern and southern Oregon, from a Portland-metro base. Most criminal and DUII cases are heard in the circuit court of the county where the offense is charged, so "where is my case?" usually comes down to which county wrote the citation or filed the charge.

## How are Oregon's trial courts organized?

Oregon's circuit courts are the state's general trial courts. Under [ORS 3.012](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_3.012), the state is divided into 27 judicial districts, each made up of one or more counties. Multnomah County, for example, is the fourth judicial district, Clackamas County the fifth and Washington County the twentieth.

Some cases can also be heard in smaller local courts:

| Court           | What it can hear (in short)                                                                                                             | Source                                                        |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Circuit court   | All crimes, including felonies, plus violations                                                                                         | [ORS 3.012](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_3.012)     |
| Justice court   | Offenses committed in its county, but not felonies or designated drug-related misdemeanors                                              | [ORS 51.050](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_51.050)   |
| Municipal court | Violations and, unless the city limits it, misdemeanors committed in the city, but not felonies or designated drug-related misdemeanors | [ORS 221.339](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_221.339) |

The court named on your citation or summons is where the case starts. Bring that paperwork to your consultation.

## Do I need a local lawyer if my case is far from Portland?

Not necessarily. Gorski Law already represents clients across the state, including eastern and southern Oregon. What changes with distance is logistics: which hearings need someone in the courtroom and which can be handled another way. That depends on the court and the type of hearing, so it is one of the first things to go over in a free consultation.

The DMV side of a DUII case is different. An [implied consent hearing](/duii-defense/dmv-implied-consent-hearing/) is run by an administrative law judge and, by default, held by telephone or another two-way electronic device, wherever the arrest happened ([ORS 813.410](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.410)).

Felony DUII in Oregon is a DUII charged as a Class C felony because of the driver's record. Under [ORS 813.011](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.011), a DUII is a felony if the person has been convicted of DUII at least twice in the 10 years before the date of the new offense. DUII is Oregon's legal term for what most states call DUI: driving under the influence of intoxicants. A second DUII is generally still a misdemeanor, but the license rules get much stricter. This page covers repeat offenses; the main [Oregon DUII defense page](/duii-defense/) covers the basics.

## When is a DUII a felony in Oregon?

Two statutes can make a DUII a Class C felony:

- **Two prior convictions in 10 years.** ORS 813.011 applies when the person has at least two DUII convictions in the 10 years before the new offense. Convictions from other jurisdictions count, including impaired-driving offenses and driving over another jurisdiction's legal blood alcohol limit.
- **Three prior convictions or adjudications in 10 years.** [ORS 813.010](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.010) also makes a DUII in a motor vehicle a Class C felony when the person has at least three prior DUII convictions, or juvenile adjudications for DUII, in the 10 years before the offense.

The 10-year window does not always apply. Once a person has been sentenced for felony DUII under ORS 813.011, any later DUII is a Class C felony "regardless of the amount of time which intervenes."

## Is a third DUI in Oregon a felony?

It depends on timing. The table shows how the new charge and the license consequence change with a person's prior DUII convictions.

| Prior DUII convictions before the new offense  | How the new DUII is charged | License after a conviction                                               |
| ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| None                                           | Class A misdemeanor         | 1-year suspension                                                        |
| One                                            | Class A misdemeanor         | 3 years if within five years of the earlier conviction; otherwise 1 year |
| Two, both within the past 10 years             | Class C felony              | Permanent revocation                                                     |
| Two, not both within the past 10 years         | Class A misdemeanor         | Permanent revocation (third conviction)                                  |
| A prior felony DUII sentence under ORS 813.011 | Class C felony, at any time | Permanent revocation                                                     |

The license column comes from [ORS 813.400](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.400), [ORS 809.428](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.428) and [ORS 809.235](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.235). A third DUII conviction leads to permanent revocation even when it is charged as a misdemeanor.

## What happens after a second DUI in Oregon?

A second DUII brings a higher minimum fine and much tighter license rules than a first:

- **Minimum fine.** At least $1,500 (ORS 813.010).
- **Mandatory sentence.** At least 48 hours in jail or 80 to 250 hours of community service ([ORS 813.020](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.020), [ORS 137.129](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_137.129)).
- **License.** A three-year suspension when the new offense and the earlier conviction fall within five years. In that case, DMV cannot issue a hardship permit ([ORS 807.240](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_807.240)).
- **Ignition interlock.** Two years after the suspension ends ([ORS 813.602](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.602)).
- **No diversion.** A DUII conviction or diversion in the past 15 years rules out diversion ([ORS 813.215](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.215)).

The DMV side is tougher too. With a DUII conviction or a diversion in the five years before the arrest, an implied consent suspension is one year for a failed test and three years for a refusal ([ORS 813.420](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.420), [ORS 813.430](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.430)). The [Oregon implied consent hearing](/duii-defense/dmv-implied-consent-hearing/) still has a 10-day request deadline.

## What are the penalties for felony DUII in Oregon?

| Consequence         | What Oregon law sets                                                     | Source                                                        |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mandatory minimum   | 90 days of incarceration for a felony under ORS 813.011, "without reduction for any reason" | ORS 813.011                                                   |
| Maximum prison term | 5 years                                                                  | [ORS 161.605](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.605) |
| Maximum fine        | $125,000                                                                 | [ORS 161.625](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.625) |
| Driving privileges  | Permanent revocation, with a petition to restore possible after 10 years | ORS 809.235                                                   |
| After restoration   | Ignition interlock for five years                                        | ORS 813.602                                                   |
| Diversion           | Not available after a felony DUII conviction under ORS 813.010(5)        | ORS 813.215                                                   |

To restore privileges, the court must find by clear and convincing evidence that the person is rehabilitated, does not pose a threat to public safety and, if the sentence required it, completed alcohol or drug treatment.

## Does a past diversion count as a prior conviction?

Not as a conviction. In diversion, the court withholds a judgment of conviction, and a completed diversion ends in dismissal ([ORS 813.230](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.230), [ORS 813.250](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.250)). The felony statutes count prior convictions. A past diversion still matters, though: one in the last 15 years bars a new diversion, and one started in the last five years lengthens an implied consent suspension.

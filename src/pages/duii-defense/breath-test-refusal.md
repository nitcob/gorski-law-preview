If you refused a breath test in Oregon after a DUII arrest, three things generally follow. DMV moves to suspend your license for one year, or three years with certain history ([ORS 813.420](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.420)). You can be cited for a separate refusal violation ([ORS 813.095](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.095)). And the refusal can be offered as evidence in the DUII case ([ORS 813.310](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.310)). DUII is Oregon's legal term for what most states call DUI: driving under the influence of intoxicants. This page is part of Gorski Law's guide to [Oregon DUII defense](/duii-defense/).

## Can you refuse a breath test in Oregon?

You can say no, but the law attaches consequences. Under Oregon's implied consent law, a person who drives on Oregon highways or premises open to the public is treated as having agreed to a breath test if arrested for DUII ([ORS 813.100](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.100)). Before the test, the officer must explain your rights and the consequences of refusing ([ORS 813.130](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.130)).

Field sobriety tests are a separate step. Police can request them when they reasonably suspect DUII ([ORS 813.135](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.135)). If you refuse to physically cooperate after being told the consequences, that refusal can also be used as evidence ([ORS 813.136](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.136)).

## What is the breath test refusal penalty in Oregon?

The consequences come from several statutes. Some are DMV actions, and some happen in court.

| Consequence                   | What the law says                                                            | Source                                                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| License suspension            | 1 year; 3 years with certain history                                         | ORS 813.420                                                                                                                  |
| Wait for a hardship permit    | 90 days; 3 years with certain history                                        | [ORS 813.520](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.520)                                                                |
| Refusal violation             | Specific fine traffic violation, $650 presumptive fine                       | ORS 813.095                                                                                                                  |
| Evidence in court             | The refusal is admissible in the DUII case                                   | ORS 813.310                                                                                                                  |
| Diversion (if allowed)        | Ignition interlock required while you have driving privileges                | [ORS 813.602](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.602)                                                                |
| Commercial driving privileges | A separate commercial suspension; no hardship permit for commercial vehicles | [ORS 813.410](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.410), [ORS 807.240](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_807.240) |

The rights-and-consequences notice describes the refusal fine as at least $500 and not more than $1,000 (ORS 813.130). The notice also says the suspension "will be substantially longer if a person refuses a test." For comparison, a first failed breath test generally leads to a 90-day suspension.

## What makes the suspension three years instead of one?

The longer suspension applies if any of these is true ([ORS 813.430](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.430)):

- You are currently in a DUII diversion or a similar alcohol or drug program.
- In the five years before the arrest, an implied consent suspension took effect against you.
- In those five years, you were convicted of DUII, in Oregon or elsewhere.
- In those five years, you started a DUII diversion or similar program.

## Can police still get a blood or urine test if you refuse?

Yes, in some situations. The implied consent statute says it does not stop police from getting a breath or blood test "through any lawful means," including a search warrant (ORS 813.100). Police may also test a person who is unconscious or otherwise unable to consent, if they have probable cause ([ORS 813.140](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.140)).

A urine test can be requested in some drug-related cases. Refusing a lawfully requested urine test is treated as refusing a breath test. That suspension runs back to back with any other implied consent suspension, and when suspensions stack this way, the wait before reinstatement or a hardship permit doubles ([ORS 813.132](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.132)). See [marijuana and drug DUII in Oregon](/duii-defense/drug-and-marijuana-duii/) for more.

## Can you challenge a refusal suspension?

Yes. You have a right to an [Oregon implied consent hearing](/duii-defense/dmv-implied-consent-hearing/), but DMV must receive your written request within 10 days of the arrest (ORS 813.410). In a refusal case, the judge looks at questions like these:

- Were you under arrest for DUII when asked to take the test?
- Did police have reasonable grounds to believe you were driving under the influence?
- Did you actually refuse?
- Were you told your rights and consequences, and given the written notice?

If the suspension is found invalid, it does not take effect. Whether to request the hearing depends on the facts. The blog post on [refusing a breath test, suspension length and hardship permits](/blog/refusing-breath-test-oregon-suspension-hardship-permit/) goes deeper.

## Can you get a hardship permit after refusing?

Possibly, after the waiting period. A hardship permit lets you drive at set times for reasons such as work, treatment, regular medical care and necessary services (ORS 807.240). You must show the need and file proof of future insurance responsibility. According to [Oregon DMV](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/driverid/hardship.aspx), DMV issues the permit only once the suspension is in effect, and a permit cannot allow more than 12 hours of driving in a day.

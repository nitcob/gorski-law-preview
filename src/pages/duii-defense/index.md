An Oregon DUII arrest sets off two separate cases. The criminal case in court decides whether you are convicted and what the sentence is. The DMV case decides whether your license is suspended under Oregon's implied consent law, and it moves faster: DMV must receive a written hearing request within 10 days of the arrest ([ORS 813.410](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.410)). DUII is Oregon's legal term for what most states call DUI: driving under the influence of intoxicants ([ORS 813.010](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.010)). For more on the name, see [DUI vs DUII in Oregon](/blog/dui-vs-duii-oregon/).

> **Deadline:** After a refused test or a failed breath test, DMV says your hearing request must be received by 11:59 pm on the tenth day after the arrest. You cannot request the hearing by phone. The [DMV implied consent hearing page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/driverid/ichearing.aspx) lists the online, fax and mail options.

## What counts as DUII in Oregon?

Under ORS 813.010, a person commits DUII by driving a vehicle while any one of these is true:

- A breath or blood test shows 0.08 percent or more alcohol in the blood.
- The person is under the influence of an intoxicant or a combination of intoxicants.
- Within two hours after driving, without drinking in between, a test shows 0.08 percent or more.

"Intoxicant" means more than alcohol. It includes cannabis, psilocybin, controlled substances, inhalants and any drug that affects a person's mental or physical faculties "to a noticeable or perceptible degree" ([ORS 801.321](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_801.321)). The law applies on highways and on any premises open to the public.

## What happens after a DUII arrest in Oregon?

Two systems handle the same arrest. They have different deciders, different deadlines and different license consequences.

| Topic | Criminal case                                                                                                                                                        | DMV implied consent case                                                                   |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| What starts it | The DUII charge                                                                                                                                                      | A refused test, or a breath or blood test at 0.08 percent or more (any amount if under 21) |
| Who decides    | A judge or jury                                                                                                                                                      | An administrative law judge                                                                |
| First deadline | Your first court date; a diversion petition is generally due within 30 days of your first appearance ([ORS 813.210](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.210)) | A written hearing request within 10 days of the arrest                                     |
| License effect | Suspension after a conviction ([ORS 813.400](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.400))                                                                        | Suspension generally starting on the 30th day after the arrest, unless the hearing finds it invalid |

The two cases do not wait for each other. The notice officers must give says "the outcome of a criminal charge for driving under the influence of intoxicants will not affect the suspension" ([ORS 813.130](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.130)). DMV also treats an implied consent suspension and a conviction suspension as separate ([Oregon DMV](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/driverid/suspreasons.aspx)).

If you had a valid Oregon license, the officer generally takes it and issues a temporary permit that lasts until the 30th day after the arrest ([ORS 813.110](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.110)).

## What are the penalties for a first DUII in Oregon?

A first DUII is generally a Class A misdemeanor. The table shows the limits and minimums the statutes set. What a court actually imposes depends on the facts of the case.

| Consequence        | What Oregon law sets                                                                                        | Source                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Maximum jail       | 364 days                                                                                                    | [ORS 161.615](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.615)                                                                |
| Maximum fine       | $6,250; up to $10,000 if a passenger under 18 (and at least 3 years younger than the driver) was in the car | [ORS 161.635](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.635), ORS 813.010                                                   |
| Minimum fine       | $1,000; $2,000 if a test showed 0.15 percent or more                                                        | ORS 813.010                                                                                                                  |
| Mandatory sentence | 48 hours in jail, or 80 to 250 hours of community service                                                   | [ORS 813.020](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.020), [ORS 137.129](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_137.129) |
| Treatment          | A screening interview ($150 fee) and the treatment program it calls for                                     | [ORS 813.021](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.021)                                                                |
| License suspension | 1 year after a first conviction                                                                             | ORS 813.400, [ORS 809.428](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.428)                                                   |
| Ignition interlock | 1 year after the suspension ends                                                                            | [ORS 813.602](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.602)                                                                |

People who meet the eligibility rules may be able to use diversion instead, a one-year program that can end with the charge dismissed. The rules are strict, and the filing deadline is short.

## Is a DUII ever a felony in Oregon?

Yes. DUII is a Class C felony when the person has been convicted of DUII at least twice in the 10 years before the new offense ([ORS 813.011](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.011)). A felony DUII conviction carries a 90-day mandatory minimum term of incarceration. It also requires the court to permanently revoke driving privileges, with a petition to restore them possible after 10 years ([ORS 809.235](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.235)).

## What should you do in the first 10 days?

These steps help keep your options open while you decide what to do next.

- Write down the date of the arrest and count 10 days forward. That is the DMV hearing deadline.
- Keep every paper the officer gave you, including the notice of intent to suspend and any temporary permit.
- Find your court date on the citation and be there. If you miss your arraignment without good cause, the court must deny diversion ([ORS 813.220](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.220)).
- Talk to a lawyer before the 10 days run out.

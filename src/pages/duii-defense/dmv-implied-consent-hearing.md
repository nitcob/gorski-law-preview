An Oregon implied consent hearing decides one question: whether DMV can validly suspend your license after a DUII arrest in which you refused a test or a breath or blood test showed too much alcohol. The request must be in writing, and DMV must receive it within 10 days of the arrest ([ORS 813.410](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.410)). DUII is Oregon's legal term for what most states call DUI: driving under the influence of intoxicants. This hearing is separate from the criminal case covered on the main [Oregon DUII defense page](/duii-defense/).

## What is Oregon's implied consent law?

Implied consent is the rule that anyone who drives a motor vehicle on Oregon highways or premises open to the public is treated as having agreed to a breath test if arrested for DUII ([ORS 813.100](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.100)). Before the test, the officer must explain your rights and the consequences ([ORS 813.130](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.130)).

If you refuse, or a breath test shows 0.08 percent or more, the officer generally takes your Oregon license and gives you a written notice of intent to suspend. If you had valid driving privileges, you also get a temporary permit that lasts until the 30th day after the arrest ([ORS 813.110](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.110)). Unless a hearing finds the suspension invalid, it starts on the 30th day after the arrest, or for a failed blood test, on the 60th day after DMV receives the report (ORS 813.410). The "fail" level is 0.04 percent if you were driving a commercial motor vehicle, and any amount of alcohol if you are under 21.

## How do you request a DMV hearing after a DUI in Oregon?

The [Oregon DMV implied consent hearing page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/driverid/ichearing.aspx) sets out the process:

- **Deadline.** For a failed breath test or any refused test, DMV must receive the request by 11:59 pm on the tenth day after the arrest. For a failed blood test, follow the number of days stated on the notice DMV mails you.
- **Not by phone.** DMV does not accept phone requests. You can request online, fax the DMV Hearings Case Management Unit at 503-945-5521, or mail it to 1905 Lana Ave NE, Salem, OR 97314.
- **What to include.** Your full name, mailing address, date of arrest, license or customer number, date of birth, a daytime phone number, a brief statement of the issues you plan to raise, your lawyer's contact details if you have one, dates you cannot attend and any interpreter needs.
- **Confirm receipt.** DMV suggests calling the Hearings Case Management Unit at 503-945-5545 to make sure it received your request.

> **Deadline:** If DMV does not receive your request in time, the suspension generally takes effect without a hearing. Late hearings are allowed only for narrow reasons, such as a verified physical incapacity, a death in the immediate family or a DMV error ([ORS 813.440](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.440)). Write down your arrest date and count 10 days.

## How long is a DUI license suspension in Oregon?

The length depends on whether you refused or failed, and on your history ([ORS 813.420](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.420)). The longer periods apply if you are in a DUII diversion now, or if in the five years before the arrest you had an implied consent suspension take effect, a DUII conviction, or started a diversion ([ORS 813.430](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.430)).

| Situation                        | Suspension | Earliest hardship permit |
| -------------------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------ |
| Failed test, no prior history    | 90 days    | After 30 days            |
| Failed test, with prior history  | 1 year     | After 1 year             |
| Refused test, no prior history   | 1 year     | After 90 days            |
| Refused test, with prior history | 3 years    | After 3 years            |

The waiting periods come from [ORS 813.520](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.520). The same section adds a 30-day wait if you were convicted of a traffic crime in the previous year, and it bars a permit if public or private transportation would meet your needs.

## What happens at the hearing?

An administrative law judge from the Office of Administrative Hearings runs the hearing by phone or another two-way electronic device. You or the officer can ask for an in-person hearing under DMV's rules. DMV must hold the hearing and issue a final order within 30 days of the arrest, or within 60 days after receiving a failed blood test report.

By law, the hearing is narrow. ORS 813.410(8) says the suspension is valid only if all of these were met:

- You were under arrest for DUII when asked to take the test.
- The police had reasonable grounds to believe you had been driving under the influence.
- You refused, or the test showed an alcohol level at or above the limit that applies to you.
- You were told your rights and consequences under ORS 813.130 and given the written notice.
- If you took a test, the person giving it was qualified, and the methods and equipment met state requirements.

DMV must issue subpoenas for witnesses you request, which can include the arresting officer. Whether to ask for a hearing is a judgment call. Read [should you request an implied consent hearing](/blog/should-you-request-implied-consent-hearing/) for the trade-offs.

## What if you lose the hearing or miss it?

If you request a hearing and then fail to appear without just cause, personally or through a lawyer, you waive it. If the judge upholds the suspension, you can appeal to circuit court within 30 days of the final order. The suspension stays in effect during the appeal.

A hardship permit may let you drive for work, treatment, regular medical care or necessary services such as grocery shopping and school runs once the waiting period ends ([ORS 807.240](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_807.240)). According to [Oregon DMV](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/driverid/hardship.aspx), the permit covers non-commercial vehicles only, requires an SR-22 insurance filing, and cannot allow more than 12 hours of driving in a day.

## Is the hearing connected to the criminal case?

They run separately. The rights notice says the outcome of the criminal charge will not affect the implied consent suspension (ORS 813.130). A DUII conviction brings its own suspension, which DMV treats as separate ([Oregon DMV](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/driverid/suspreasons.aspx)).

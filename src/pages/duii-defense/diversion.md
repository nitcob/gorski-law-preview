Oregon DUII diversion lets an eligible person charged with DUII complete a one-year program instead of going to trial or being sentenced. The person pleads guilty or no contest, but the judge withholds a judgment of conviction ([ORS 813.230](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.230)). If the person fully complies, the court can dismiss the charge with prejudice ([ORS 813.250](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.250)). DUII is Oregon's legal term for what most states call DUI: driving under the influence of intoxicants. Diversion is one option within [Oregon DUII defense](/duii-defense/), and it is not right for every case.

## How does the DUII diversion program in Oregon work?

The court must tell a person charged with DUII at arraignment that diversion may be available ([ORS 813.200](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.200)). From there, the process generally runs like this:

1. **File the petition.** It includes a signed plea of guilty or no contest and a sworn statement that you meet the eligibility rules. The court's official form is the [DUII Diversion Petition and Agreement](https://www.courts.oregon.gov/forms/Documents/DUII_Diversion_Form_1_Petition_Agreement.pdf).
2. **Serve the prosecutor.** The district attorney or city attorney has 15 days after service to object and ask for a hearing ([ORS 813.210](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.210)).
3. **The court decides.** The judge considers whether diversion will benefit you and the community ([ORS 813.220](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.220)).
4. **Complete the conditions.** These include a screening interview, any treatment it calls for, no use of intoxicants, and an ignition interlock device if required.
5. **Ask for dismissal.** After the one-year period, a person who fully complied can move to dismiss the charge.

## Do I qualify for DUII diversion?

[ORS 813.215](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.215) sets the eligibility rules. In plain terms, you generally qualify only if all of these are true:

- No other DUII charge is pending against you, in Oregon or elsewhere.
- No DUII conviction in the 15 years before the offense (through the date you file).
- No DUII diversion or similar alcohol or drug program in those 15 years, and none in progress.
- Never convicted of felony DUII under [ORS 813.010](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.010)(5).
- No pending charge, and no conviction in the past 15 years, for aggravated vehicular homicide, murder, manslaughter, criminally negligent homicide or assault resulting from driving.
- You did not hold commercial driving privileges on the offense date, and you were not driving a commercial motor vehicle.
- The offense did not involve a crash that killed anyone or caused physical injury to anyone other than you.

The court must also deny the petition if you missed your arraignment without good cause, or if you pick up a new DUII charge after filing ([ORS 813.220](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.220)). The blog post on [Oregon DUII diversion requirements](/blog/oregon-duii-diversion-requirements/) walks through these rules with examples.

## When is the diversion petition due?

The petition must generally be filed within 30 days after your first appearance on the summons ([ORS 813.210](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.210)). A court can allow a later date for good cause, but the statute says filing a demurrer, a motion to suppress or a motion for an omnibus hearing does not count as good cause.

The petition cannot be filed after a guilty or no contest plea is entered, or after a trial on the charge has started. One exception applies to some drug DUII cases: where the charge alleges a controlled substance or inhalant, an officer took a blood or urine sample, and there is no known alcohol result of 0.08 percent or more, the petition may be filed up to 14 days after the prosecutor sends the lab results.

> **Deadline:** The 30-day clock starts at your first court appearance, not when you hire a lawyer. Note the date on your paperwork.

## Does diversion stop the DMV license suspension?

No. Diversion resolves the criminal case. The implied consent suspension at DMV is separate, and the law says the outcome of the criminal charge will not affect it ([ORS 813.130](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.130)). The [Oregon implied consent hearing](/duii-defense/dmv-implied-consent-hearing/) has its own 10-day deadline.

Diversion also brings license-related conditions:

- **Ignition interlock.** The court must require one during diversion whenever you have driving privileges if a test showed 0.08 percent or more, if you refused a test, or if a test showed some alcohol plus another intoxicant ([ORS 813.602](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.602)). A medical exemption may be available.
- **Driving record.** DMV adds the diversion agreement to your driving record (ORS 813.230).
- **New arrests.** A person who is in diversion, or who started one in the past five years, faces a longer implied consent suspension after a new arrest ([ORS 813.430](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.430)).

## What happens if you cannot finish diversion?

If you need more time, you can ask for one extension of up to 180 days, filed within 30 days before the period ends. The court can grant it if it finds you made a good faith effort and can finish in the extra time ([ORS 813.225](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.225)).

If the court finds you broke the agreement or no longer qualify, it terminates diversion, enters the plea you already signed, and sentences you ([ORS 813.255](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.255)). Committing a new DUII during the diversion period violates the agreement (ORS 813.200).

A marijuana DUI in Oregon is a DUII charge that says the driver was under the influence of cannabis instead of, or along with, alcohol. Oregon law sets a 0.08 percent limit for alcohol, but it sets no comparable number for THC or other drugs ([ORS 813.010](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.010)). So in a drug DUII case, the State generally has to prove the driver was actually under the influence. DUII is Oregon's legal term for what most states call DUI: driving under the influence of intoxicants. For the bigger picture, see the main [Oregon DUII defense page](/duii-defense/).

## What counts as an intoxicant in Oregon?

Oregon defines "intoxicant" broadly ([ORS 801.321](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_801.321)). It includes:

- Alcohol
- Cannabis
- Psilocybin
- Controlled substances
- Inhalants
- Any other drug that, alone or combined with these, "adversely affects a person's mental or physical faculties to a noticeable or perceptible degree"

That definition came from [House Bill 2316](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2023R1/Measures/Overview/HB2316), passed by the Legislature in 2023. A person commits DUII by driving while under the influence of any intoxicant or a combination of them.

## Is there a legal THC limit for driving in Oregon?

No. ORS 813.010 sets a 0.08 percent blood alcohol number. It does not set a nanogram or blood-level limit for cannabis or any other drug. A drug DUII charge rests on the second part of the statute: being "under the influence of an intoxicant." That makes the evidence of impairment the center of the case. The blog post on [how marijuana impairment is proven in Oregon](/blog/marijuana-drug-duii-oregon-how-impairment-is-proven/) covers the kinds of evidence involved.

## How do police test for drugs after a DUII arrest?

Oregon law allows several kinds of tests. Each has its own rules.

| Test                 | When the law allows it                                                                                             | Source                                                        |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Field sobriety tests | When an officer reasonably suspects DUII                                                                           | [ORS 813.135](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.135) |
| Urine test           | After a DUII arrest, if a breath test shows under 0.08 percent or there was a crash with injury or property damage | [ORS 813.131](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.131) |
| Blood or urine test  | With your express consent, or if you are unconscious or unable to consent and police have probable cause           | [ORS 813.140](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.140) |
| Breath or blood, other means | Through any lawful means, including a search warrant                                                                                         | [ORS 813.100](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.100) |
| Your own test        | At your own expense, by a qualified person you choose                                                              | [ORS 813.150](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.150) |

The urine test has extra limits. The officer must have at least eight hours of state-certified training in recognizing drug-impaired driving and must reasonably suspect a non-alcohol intoxicant. You must be given privacy and may not be watched by an officer while giving the sample. The statute treats a urine analysis as valid when an accredited or licensed toxicology lab performs it.

## What happens if you refuse a urine test?

A refusal of a urine test requested under ORS 813.131 is treated like a breath test refusal: generally a one-year suspension, or three years with certain history. That suspension runs back to back with any other implied consent suspension ([ORS 813.132](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.132)). There is an exception if a physician licensed in Oregon documents a medical condition that makes giving a sample impossible. See [what happens after refusing a breath test in Oregon](/duii-defense/breath-test-refusal/) for the full refusal picture.

## Can you get a DUI for prescription drugs in Oregon?

Yes. A legally prescribed drug can be an intoxicant if it affects your faculties to a noticeable or perceptible degree (ORS 801.321). Some prescription medicines are also controlled substances under Oregon law ([ORS 475.005](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_475.005)).

Oregon does offer a narrow affirmative defense ([ORS 813.015](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.015)). It can apply when all of these are true:

- You got the drug with a valid prescription, or it was available without one.
- You took it at the prescribed or recommended dose and followed all directions and warnings.
- You had a reaction you could not reasonably have anticipated, which affected your faculties while driving.

The defense does not apply when the charge pleads alcohol, cannabis, psilocybin, an inhalant or a controlled substance. You must also file written notice of the defense at least 45 days before the first trial date, unless the parties agree otherwise or the court allows a later date for good cause. Even then, the notice can be no later than 21 days before trial.

> **Note:** A drug DUII charge that relies on a controlled substance or inhalant must say so in the charging document, and that fact must be proved at trial or admitted in a guilty plea (ORS 813.010).

## How does a drug DUII affect diversion and your license?

- **Diversion deadline.** In some drug cases, the petition may be filed up to 14 days after the prosecutor sends the blood or urine lab results ([ORS 813.210](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.210)). See [Oregon DUII diversion](/duii-defense/diversion/).
- **Ignition interlock.** In diversion, the court must require one if a test showed some alcohol plus another intoxicant, and may require one in other cases ([ORS 813.602](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.602)).
- **DMV suspension.** The implied consent suspension at DMV is tied to a refusal or to an alcohol test result ([ORS 813.410](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.410)). A DUII conviction based on drugs still brings a license suspension ([ORS 813.400](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.400)).

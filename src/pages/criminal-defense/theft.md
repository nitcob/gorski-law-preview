In Oregon, theft means taking, obtaining or keeping someone else's property with the intent to deprive the owner of it or to appropriate it ([ORS 164.015](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_164.015)). The degree of the charge depends mostly on the value of the property. Shoplifting is charged as theft, so the same dollar lines apply to a store case as to any other.

## How does Oregon grade theft by value?

The table shows the four degrees of theft by value, with the statutory maximum penalties for each class.

| Charge                                                                             | Value: thefts before Jan. 1, 2027 | Value: thefts on or after Jan. 1, 2027 | Class               | Maximum jail or prison | Maximum fine |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------ |
| Theft III ([ORS 164.043](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_164.043))          | Less than $100                    | Less than $150                         | Class C misdemeanor | 30 days                | $1,250       |
| Theft II ([ORS 164.045](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_164.045))           | $100 to less than $1,000          | $150 to less than $1,500               | Class A misdemeanor | 364 days               | $6,250       |
| Theft I ([ORS 164.055](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_164.055))            | $1,000 or more                    | $1,500 or more                         | Class C felony      | 5 years                | $125,000     |
| Aggravated Theft I ([ORS 164.057](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_164.057)) | $10,000 or more                   | $10,000 or more                        | Class B felony      | 10 years               | $250,000     |

Maximums: [ORS 161.605](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.605), [ORS 161.615](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.615), [ORS 161.625](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.625) and [ORS 161.635](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.635). These are ceilings, not typical sentences. Aggravated Theft I does not apply to a motor vehicle used mainly for personal transportation.

> **Note:** A 2026 law, [House Bill 4041](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2026R1/Measures/Overview/HB4041), raises the dollar lines for Theft III, Theft II and Theft I to $150 and $1,500. The new amounts become operative January 1, 2027, and apply only to thefts committed on or after that date ([Oregon Laws 2026, chapter 14](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/lawsstatutes/2026orLaw0014.pdf), sections 13 to 15 and 18). The class of each charge and the $10,000 line for Aggravated Theft I do not change.

## When is theft a felony no matter the value?

Theft in the first degree in Oregon also covers theft of lower-value property in these situations ([ORS 164.055](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_164.055)):

- The theft happens during a riot, fire, explosion, catastrophe or other emergency, in the affected area.
- It is theft by receiving, committed by buying, selling, borrowing or lending on the security of the property.
- The property is a firearm or an explosive.
- The property is a livestock animal, a companion dog or cat, or certain wild animals.
- The property is a "precursor substance" as defined in Oregon's drug laws.
- During the theft, the person recklessly creates a substantial risk of serious physical injury to another person.

A separate crime, organized retail theft, applies when a person acting with someone else takes merchandise from a store and the total taken within any 180-day period is more than $5,000. It is a Class B felony ([ORS 164.098](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_164.098)).

## How is the value of stolen property decided?

Value usually decides the degree, so how it is measured matters ([ORS 164.115](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_164.115)):

- Value generally means market value at the time and place of the theft. If that cannot reasonably be found, it means the cost to replace the property within a reasonable time.
- If the value cannot reasonably be determined at all, the law presumes it was less than $100.
- The values of separate thefts committed within one year, against the same or different victims, may be added together.

## What happens after a shoplifting stop?

A shoplifting case can involve three separate things at once.

1. **The stop.** A merchant or employee who has probable cause to believe a person stole from the store may detain the person in a reasonable manner and for a reasonable time ([ORS 131.655](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_131.655)).
2. **The criminal charge.** A theft charge may be filed, graded by value as shown above.
3. **The civil demand.** Separately, Oregon law makes an adult who shoplifts civilly liable to the store for actual damages, a penalty equal to the retail value of the merchandise up to $500, and an additional penalty of $100 to $250. A criminal conviction is not required, and the store may demand payment in writing before it sues ([ORS 30.875](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_30.875)).

> **Note:** A civil demand letter is a written request for payment made before any lawsuit is filed. It is not a court summons, and the civil claim is separate from any criminal charge.

If a person is convicted of a crime that caused economic damages, the court generally must also order restitution for the full amount ([ORS 137.106](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors137.html)).

## Are there options for a first-time theft charge?

Oregon law includes two paths that can end some cases without a conviction. Both depend on the district attorney, and neither is automatic.

- **Diversion.** A district attorney may offer diversion after weighing listed factors. The offense generally must not have involved physical injury, and diversion cannot be offered to someone who has done diversion before ([ORS 135.886](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_135.886)). If the person completes the program, the charges are dismissed with prejudice; there is a $100 program fee ([ORS 135.891](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_135.891)).
- **Probation without a judgment of guilt.** For a misdemeanor that did not involve injury, a person with no prior convictions who pleads guilty or is found guilty can, on the district attorney's motion and with the person's consent, be placed on probation without a judgment of guilt being entered. Completing it ends in dismissal, and it can be used only once ([ORS 137.533](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_137.533)).

For Theft I, a Class C felony, a judge may enter the conviction as a Class A misdemeanor if a felony conviction would be unduly harsh ([ORS 161.705](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.705)).

In Oregon, possessing a small amount of a controlled substance is a drug enforcement misdemeanor. [HB 4002](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2024R1/Measures/Overview/HB4002) created that crime for conduct on or after September 1, 2024. Larger amounts are Class A misdemeanors, and possession can be a felony when the amount is a "substantial quantity" or the case is a "commercial drug offense." The same law created a conditional discharge path that can end in dismissal.

## What changed with HB 4002?

HB 4002 took effect when the Governor signed it on April 1, 2024. Its possession changes became operative on September 1, 2024, and apply to conduct on or after that date ([HB 4002 enrolled text, section 73](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2024R1/Downloads/MeasureDocument/HB4002/Enrolled)).

Before then, possessing a small amount was a Class E violation. A violation is not a crime ([ORS 161.505](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.505)). HB 4002 replaced it with the drug enforcement misdemeanor, set a special sentence for it, created a new conditional discharge, and encouraged police and prosecutors to use deflection programs.

In 2025, [SB 236](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2025R1/Measures/Overview/SB236) gave fentanyl its own possession, delivery and manufacture statutes and adjusted conditional discharge procedures. It took effect July 17, 2025.

## How serious is a drug possession charge in Oregon?

For the drugs below, the amount sets the level of the charge. The table shows the lines drawn in the official 2025 statutes ([ORS chapter 475](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors475.html)).

| Drug (statute)                                       | Drug enforcement misdemeanor                 | Class A misdemeanor                     | Felony (substantial quantity)                             |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Heroin ([ORS 475.854](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors475.html))                                 | Less than 1 gram                             | 1 gram or more                          | 5 grams or more: Class B felony                           |
| Fentanyl ([ORS 475.794](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors475.html))                               | Less than 1 gram and fewer than 5 user units | 1 gram or more, or 5 or more user units | 5 grams or more, or 25 or more user units: Class C felony |
| Cocaine ([ORS 475.884](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors475.html))                                | Less than 2 grams                            | 2 grams or more                         | 10 grams or more: Class C felony                          |
| Methamphetamine ([ORS 475.894](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors475.html))                        | Less than 2 grams                            | 2 grams or more                         | 10 grams or more: Class C felony                          |
| MDMA ([ORS 475.874](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors475.html))                                   | Less than 1 gram and fewer than 5 pills      | 1 gram or more, or 5 or more pills      | 5 grams or more, or 25 or more pills: Class B felony      |
| Oxycodone without a valid prescription ([ORS 475.834](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors475.html)) | Fewer than 40 pills or user units            | 40 or more pills or user units          | No quantity line; Class C felony only as a commercial drug offense |

Substantial quantities are listed in [ORS 475.900](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors475.html). Possessing other Schedule I to IV controlled substances is generally a drug enforcement misdemeanor, with exceptions for larger amounts, and Schedule V possession is a violation ([ORS 475.752](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors475.html)).

> **Warning:** A "commercial drug offense" can make possession a felony even when the amount is small. It requires at least three listed factors, such as $300 or more in cash, packaging materials like scales, drug transaction records or customer lists, or stolen property (ORS 475.900).

Selling, or intending to sell, can be charged as delivery, a separate and more serious crime. Oregon defines "delivery" to include possession with the intent to transfer ([ORS 475.005](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors475.html)), and delivery of methamphetamine or fentanyl is a Class B felony ([ORS 475.890](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors475.html), [ORS 475.790](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors475.html)).

## What is the sentence for a drug enforcement misdemeanor in Oregon?

The drug enforcement misdemeanor has its own sentencing rules ([ORS 475.896](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors475.html)):

- The court may impose up to 180 days in jail only if the defendant asks for that sentence.
- Otherwise, the court must impose supervised probation of up to 18 months, and jail cannot be a condition of probation.
- The judgment cannot require a fine, cost, assessment or attorney fee.
- Probation violations can bring sanctions, but jail sanctions are capped at 30 days in total. If probation is revoked, the court can impose up to 180 days and must authorize early release to a treatment program.

## What is conditional discharge, and how do you ask for it?

Conditional discharge is a probation agreement that can end with the possession charge dismissed ([ORS 475.897](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors475.html)).

- **Who can ask:** a person charged with possession that is a drug enforcement misdemeanor. The district attorney may object, and the court may deny entry if the agreement would not serve the person's needs or the community's protection.
- **When:** no later than 30 days after the first appearance, unless the court allows a later date for good cause. Filing a demurrer, a motion to suppress or a motion for an omnibus hearing does not count as good cause.
- **The terms:** 12 months of probation, which the court can end early, including a substance use evaluation and any treatment the evaluator recommends. No guilty plea is required, but the agreement waives trial rights, including a jury trial.
- **If it goes wrong:** the court may impose a sanction or resume the case. If the case resumes, the person cannot contest whether the evidence is enough to prove guilt.
- **If it goes right:** the court dismisses the charge. The dismissal is not a conviction.

> **Deadline:** The request is due within 30 days after the first court appearance. Talk to a lawyer early.

## What is deflection?

A deflection program is a partnership between law enforcement and behavioral health or community organizations that connects people who may have a substance use disorder with treatment, recovery support, housing or other services ([ORS 137.687](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors137.html)). HB 4002 encourages police to refer people suspected of a drug enforcement misdemeanor to deflection instead of citing or arresting them, or after a citation or arrest but before the case goes to the district attorney. It also encourages district attorneys to divert these cases to assessment and treatment ([HB 4002, section 36](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2024R1/Downloads/MeasureDocument/HB4002/Enrolled)). The law encourages deflection but does not require it, and whether a program exists depends on where the case is. A citation court date can be set more than 30 days out to allow deflection ([ORS 133.060](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors133.html)).

If you are trying to work out what to do after a DUI arrest in Oregon, start with the calendar. If you refused a test or failed a breath test, Oregon DMV must receive your written request for an implied consent hearing within 10 days of the arrest. If it does not, your license suspension starts on the 30th day after the arrest ([ORS 813.410](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.410)). Separately, the criminal case has its own court date, and missing it causes problems of its own.

DUII is Oregon's legal term for what most states call DUI: driving under the influence of intoxicants ([ORS 813.010](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.010)). One arrest starts two cases. One is a DMV case about your license. The other is a criminal case in court. They run on different clocks, and the first 10 days matter most for the DMV case. This post walks through those days in order. It is general information, not advice about your situation.

## Why does the 10-day deadline matter so much?

Oregon's implied consent law says that anyone who drives a motor vehicle on Oregon highways, or on premises open to the public, is treated as having agreed to a breath test if arrested for DUII ([ORS 813.100](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.100)). If you refuse, or if a breath test shows a blood alcohol level of 0.08 percent or more, the officer takes your Oregon license, gives you a written notice of intent to suspend and reports to DMV. For drivers under 21, any amount of alcohol counts as a failed test for this purpose ([ORS 813.300](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.300)).

That report starts a DMV suspension. The way to challenge it before it begins is an implied consent hearing in front of an administrative law judge. Under ORS 813.410, you have 10 days from the date of arrest to get your request to DMV. If you failed a blood test rather than a breath test, the 10 days run from the date DMV sends you its notice of suspension instead.

Oregon DMV's [implied consent hearing page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/driverid/ichearing.aspx) is specific. For a breath test failure or any refusal, the request must be **received** at DMV headquarters by 11:59 pm on the tenth day after the arrest. Mailing it on day 10 is not enough. DMV also says a request that arrives on a weekend or holiday counts as received on the next business day.

> **Deadline:** Count 10 days from the date of arrest and write that date down today. If day 10 falls on a weekend or holiday, plan to get the request in earlier.

## How do you request the DMV hearing?

You cannot request the hearing by calling DMV. According to DMV, you can:

- submit the online request form linked from DMV's implied consent hearing page;
- fax your request to the DMV Hearings Case Management Unit at 503-945-5521; or
- mail it to DMV Hearings Case Management Unit, 1905 Lana Ave NE, Salem OR 97314.

DMV says the request must be in writing and include your full name, mailing address, date of arrest, Oregon license, permit, ID card or customer number (if you have one), date of birth, a daytime phone number, a brief statement of the issues you plan to raise, your lawyer's name and contact details (if you have one), dates you or your lawyer cannot attend, and an interpreter language if you need one. DMV suggests calling the Hearings Case Management Unit at 503-945-5545 to make sure your request arrived.

Whether to ask for a hearing at all is a real decision with tradeoffs. We cover it in [should you request an implied consent hearing?](/blog/should-you-request-implied-consent-hearing/), and our page on the [Oregon implied consent hearing](/duii-defense/dmv-implied-consent-hearing/) explains the process.

## What did the officer give you, and what does it mean?

- **Notice of intent to suspend.** The officer must give you this written notice ([ORS 813.100](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.100)). By law, the information must state how many days you have to request a hearing and how many days until the hearing will be held ([ORS 813.130](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.130)).
- **Temporary driving permit.** If you qualify, the officer issues one on DMV's behalf. It is valid until the 30th day after the arrest and grants the same privileges as the license that was taken ([ORS 813.110](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.110)). It does not take effect right away; the information you were given must state how many hours until it does. No permit is issued if your privileges were already suspended or revoked, if you were driving on an invalid license, or if you hold an out-of-state license that was not taken.
- **Paperwork about the criminal charge.** Keep any citation or release papers and find your first court date.
- **Test report.** If you took a breath or blood test, the person who has the written test report must give you or your lawyer a copy on request ([ORS 813.160](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.160)).

## When does the suspension start, and how long does it last?

If DMV gets a proper report and there is no hearing that finds the suspension invalid, the suspension starts on the 30th day after the arrest. For a failed blood test, it starts on the 60th day after DMV receives the report ([ORS 813.410](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.410)). Your temporary permit runs out on day 30, so the two dates line up.

The length depends on why you were suspended and on your history ([ORS 813.420](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.420)). The longer periods apply if you are now in a DUII diversion program, or if in the five years before the arrest you had an implied consent suspension take effect, a DUII conviction, or started a diversion program ([ORS 813.430](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.430)).

| Reason for suspension    | Length without that history | Length with that history |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Failed breath/blood test | 90 days                     | 1 year                   |
| Refused a test           | 1 year                      | 3 years                  |

Some drivers can apply for a hardship permit after a waiting period. Without that history, the wait is 30 days into a suspension for a failed test and 90 days for a refusal ([ORS 813.520](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.520)).

If you hold a commercial driver license, ORS 813.410 also provides for suspending your commercial driving privileges, and a 0.04 percent level applies if you were driving a commercial motor vehicle. Our page for [commercial drivers](/traffic-tickets/cdl-commercial-drivers/) covers that side.

## What happens with the criminal case in the meantime?

The court case moves on its own track. The DMV suspension does not wait for it: the rights-and-consequences information officers give says the outcome of a criminal DUII charge will not affect the suspension ([ORS 813.130](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.130)). A first DUII is a Class A misdemeanor with a minimum $1,000 fine on conviction ([ORS 813.010](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.010)).

At arraignment, the court must tell you that a diversion agreement may be available if you qualify ([ORS 813.200](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.200)). Missing that hearing matters: the court must deny a diversion petition if you failed to appear at arraignment without good cause ([ORS 813.220](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.220)).

> **Warning:** Do not miss your court date. Put it on your calendar the day you find it.

## When do you need to decide about diversion?

Diversion is a one-year agreement that can end with the DUII charge dismissed if you complete every condition. The petition is due within 30 days after your first court appearance, unless the court allows a later date for good cause. It cannot be filed after you plead guilty or no contest, or after trial starts ([ORS 813.210](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.210)). The filing fee is $490, plus $150 paid to the agency that does the screening interview ([ORS 813.240](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.240)).

For most people that deadline falls after the first 10 days, but it is worth knowing now. Our guide to [DUII diversion requirements in Oregon](/blog/oregon-duii-diversion-requirements/) explains who qualifies, and the [Oregon DUII diversion](/duii-defense/diversion/) page covers how the program works.

## What goes on this week's checklist?

- Write down your 10-day date and your first court date.
- Keep the notice of intent to suspend, the temporary permit and all court paperwork together.
- Decide, ideally with a lawyer, whether to request the implied consent hearing. If you do, get the written request to DMV well before the deadline and call 503-945-5545 to confirm it arrived.
- Ask for a copy of your breath or blood test report.
- Write down what you remember about the stop, the test and the times, while it is fresh.
- If you hold a CDL, flag that early. Commercial privileges follow their own rules.
- Talk to a lawyer before day 10 if you can.

## What if you already missed the 10 days?

DMV can hold a late hearing only in limited situations, such as a physician-verified incapacity, a death in your immediate family, a DMV error, or other just cause defined by DMV rule ([ORS 813.440](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.440)). Even when DMV grants a late hearing, the suspension generally still starts on schedule unless that hearing finds it invalid.

A single routine ticket usually will not take away a commercial driver license (CDL) by itself. But a CDL ticket can still lose you a job in Oregon. Federal rules disqualify drivers after a second "serious traffic violation" within three years, count many tickets you get in your own car, bar states from hiding convictions through diversion, and require you to tell your employer about most traffic convictions within 30 days.

For people who drive for a living, the fine is rarely the real cost. The record is. Here is how the rules work, with links to the federal regulations and Oregon statutes behind them.

## Can one ticket disqualify a CDL?

Usually not, if it is a single ordinary ticket. Federal rules sort CDL offenses into tiers, and each tier has its own disqualification periods ([49 CFR 383.51](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-383.51)). Oregon applies similar rules, and in places stricter ones, through its own statutes ([ORS 809.510](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.510), [ORS 809.525](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.525)).

| Tier                         | Examples                                                                                                                                  | First conviction (CDL)             | Repeat convictions                                                        |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Serious traffic violation    | Speeding 15 mph or more over the limit, reckless driving, improper lane changes, following too closely, a violation tied to a fatal crash | No disqualification                | 60 days for a second within 3 years; 120 days for a third within 3 years  |
| Major offense                | Driving under the influence, refusing an alcohol test, leaving the scene of an accident, using a vehicle to commit a felony               | 1 year (3 years if hauling hazmat) | Lifetime for a second; some drivers can seek reinstatement after 10 years |
| Railroad crossing (in a CMV) | Failing to slow, stop or check for trains when required                                                                                   | 60 days                            | 120 days for a second within 3 years; 1 year for a third                  |

A "CMV" is a commercial motor vehicle. Under federal rules that includes vehicles used in commerce that are rated at 26,001 pounds or more (and certain combinations), vehicles designed to carry 16 or more people counting the driver, and vehicles of any size hauling hazardous materials that require placards ([49 CFR 383.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-383.5)).

In Oregon, a lifetime CDL suspension after a second major offense cannot be lifted any earlier than 10 years, and only under DMV rules ([ORS 809.520](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors809.html#:~:text=809.520%20Lifetime%20suspension%20of%20commercial%20driving%20privileges%3B%20rules.)).

## Do tickets in your personal vehicle count?

Yes, many do. The federal disqualification rules apply when a CDL holder "drives a CMV or non-CMV" and is convicted (49 CFR 383.51).

- **Major offenses count in any vehicle.** A DUII, a test refusal, leaving the scene or a felony committed with your own pickup carries at least the same one-year federal disqualification as one in a truck. (Oregon's suspension for a refusal is longer; see below.)
- **Some serious violations count in any vehicle.** A serious violation in a personal vehicle counts toward the 60- and 120-day periods when the conviction leads to a suspension of your license or driving privileges. Oregon DMV lists these personal-vehicle serious offenses as reckless driving, speeding at 100 mph or more, and speeding 30 mph or more over the limit when the court imposes a suspension ([Oregon DMV](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/DMV/Pages/DriverID/federal_CDL_reqs.aspx)).
- **Failing a breath or blood test at 0.08 or more in any vehicle** triggers a one-year suspension of Oregon commercial driving privileges (ORS 809.510).

The employer notice rule, covered below, also reaches convictions "in any type of motor vehicle."

## What is the 0.04 limit for commercial drivers?

Oregon's criminal DUII limit is 0.08 percent for every driver ([ORS 813.010](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.010)). Commercial drivers face a lower line too. If you are driving a commercial motor vehicle, a breath or blood test of 0.04 percent or more is a failed test under Oregon's implied consent law ([ORS 813.410](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.410)). That leads to a one-year CDL suspension, or three years if you were hauling hazardous materials (ORS 809.510). The federal disqualification table uses the same 0.04 figure (49 CFR 383.51).

Even below 0.04, an officer must put a CMV driver out of service for 24 hours if a breath test shows any alcohol at all. The same applies if the officer has reasonable grounds to believe the driver drank within four hours before driving. Violating that order is a Class A misdemeanor ([ORS 813.050](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.050)).

## Can a CDL holder get diversion or have a ticket hidden?

No. In Oregon you are not eligible for DUII diversion if you held commercial driving privileges on the date of the offense, or if you were driving a commercial motor vehicle ([ORS 813.215](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.215)). Oregon DMV goes further: CDL and commercial learner permit holders "are not eligible for any diversions for traffic offenses" (Oregon DMV).

This comes from the federal ban on "masking." A state must not mask a conviction, defer judgment, or allow a diversion program that would keep a CDL holder's conviction off the national CDL driver record ([49 CFR 384.226](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-384.226)). It covers traffic violations in any type of vehicle, other than parking, vehicle weight and vehicle defect violations.

You may still count as a CDL holder even if your CDL has expired or been suspended. Oregon DMV treats you as one if your most recently issued license is, or was, a commercial license. The main exception is if you voluntarily surrendered it.

## Does paying the ticket count as a conviction?

Yes. The federal definition of "conviction" is broad. It includes a guilty or no contest plea accepted by the court, a forfeited bail, and "the payment of a fine or court cost" (49 CFR 383.5). Paying a ticket by mail is a conviction for CDL purposes.

That is why it helps to know what a conviction will mean before you pay. Our post on [whether it is worth fighting a speeding ticket in Oregon](/blog/is-it-worth-fighting-speeding-ticket-oregon/) walks through the options and their trade-offs.

## Will your employer find out?

Usually, yes, and often from you.

- **You must tell them.** A CDL holder convicted of a traffic-control violation in any type of vehicle, other than parking, must notify the current employer in writing within 30 days of the conviction ([49 CFR 383.31](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-383.31)). The notice lists the offense, any suspension that resulted, whether you were in a CMV, and where it happened.
- **They check your record.** Motor carriers must pull each driver's motor vehicle record at least once every 12 months. They must give "great weight" to violations such as speeding, reckless driving and driving under the influence ([49 CFR 391.25](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-391.25)).
- **They cannot let a disqualified driver drive.** An employer must not knowingly allow a disqualified driver to operate a CMV (49 CFR 383.51).

The federal rules do not decide whether an employer keeps a driver after a conviction. That is up to the employer. But a disqualification means you cannot drive a CMV at all for that period.

## What happens if a CDL holder refuses a breath test?

In Oregon, refusing a test while driving any motor vehicle leads to a three-year suspension of your commercial driving privileges. It is five years if you were driving a commercial motor vehicle carrying hazardous materials (ORS 809.510). The federal table sets a one-year disqualification for a first refusal (49 CFR 383.51), so Oregon's period is longer.

A hardship permit cannot authorize driving a commercial motor vehicle ([ORS 807.240](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_807.240)). Our post on [breath test refusal suspensions and hardship permits](/blog/refusing-breath-test-oregon-suspension-hardship-permit/) covers the effect on your regular license.

## Who do these rules hit hardest?

Anyone whose paycheck depends on a CDL. That includes truck drivers, school bus drivers, and construction and landscaping workers who drive heavy trucks or haul equipment. A vehicle used in commerce that is designed to carry 16 or more people, counting the driver, is a CMV under the federal definition (49 CFR 383.5).

Because many rules reach personal vehicles, a weekend ticket in the family car can follow you to work on Monday. If you drive for a living without a CDL, such as many taxi drivers, these federal CDL rules may not apply to you. Your ordinary driving record still matters, though, and our post on [fighting a speeding ticket](/blog/is-it-worth-fighting-speeding-ticket-oregon/) explains Oregon DMV's Driver Improvement Program.

## What can you do if you get a ticket?

General steps that apply to most CDL holders:

- **Do not miss the court date.** Oregon DMV suspends a CDL for failing to appear in court or failing to pay a traffic fine, in any state.
- **Read the exact charge and speed.** In a CMV, 15 mph or more over the limit is a serious violation. 14 mph over is not on that list.
- **Count back three years** for any earlier serious violation convictions.
- **Remember that paying is a conviction** under the federal definition.
- **If you are convicted, notify your employer in writing within 30 days.**
- **Talk to a lawyer before you plead,** so you understand how the conviction would count.

Oregon puts most traffic tickets into one of four classes, A through D, and the class sets the fine. Here is the short version of Oregon traffic violation classes, fines and limits: the presumptive fine printed on a ticket is $440 for Class A, $265 for Class B, $165 for Class C and $115 for Class D ([ORS 153.019](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.019%20Presumptive%20fines%3B%20generally.)), and the legal maximum runs from $250 up to $2,000 ([ORS 153.018](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.018%20Maximum%20fines.)). Fines are higher when the officer notes that the offense happened in a highway work zone, a posted school zone or a safety corridor.

This post explains what each number means, how speed decides the class of a speeding ticket, and what happens if a ticket is ignored.

## What are the classes of traffic violations in Oregon?

Oregon law sorts violations into six categories for sentencing: Class A, Class B, Class C, Class D, unclassified and specific fine violations ([ORS 153.012](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.012%20Violation%20categories.)). Class A is the most serious and Class D the least.

- **Unclassified violation.** A violation whose statute does not name a class. It is treated as a Class B violation ([ORS 153.015](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_153.015)).
- **Specific fine violation.** An offense that carries its own set fine instead of a class, such as driving 100 mph or more.

A violation is not a crime. The penalty is a fine, and the law creating a violation cannot add jail time (ORS 153.018). A violation conviction also does not carry the legal disabilities of a criminal conviction ([ORS 153.008](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_153.008)). It can still go on your driving record and lead to license consequences, as explained below.

Older guides may mention a Class E violation. Measure 110 created it for small-amount drug possession ([SB 755 staff measure summary, 2021](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2021R1/Downloads/MeasureAnalysisDocument/62427)), and House Bill 4002 removed it from ORS 153.012 as of September 1, 2024 ([HB 4002 enrolled text](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2024R1/Downloads/MeasureDocument/HB4002/Enrolled), sections 59 and 73). Our post on [Oregon drug possession law after HB 4002](/blog/drug-possession-oregon-after-hb-4002/) covers that change.

## How much is the fine for each class?

These are the amounts for an individual under the 2025 edition of the Oregon Revised Statutes. A corporation faces higher maximums (ORS 153.018).

| Class         | Presumptive fine (on the ticket) | Minimum fine a court must impose | Maximum fine                 | Presumptive fine in a work zone, school zone or safety corridor |
| ------------- | -------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A             | $440                             | $225                             | $2,000                       | $875                                                            |
| B             | $265                             | $135                             | $1,000                       | $525                                                            |
| C             | $165                             | $85                              | $500                         | $325                                                            |
| D             | $115                             | $65                              | $250                         | $225                                                            |
| Specific fine | Set by the statute               | Set by the statute               | $2,000 unless set by statute | Not applicable                                                  |

Sources: ORS 153.018, ORS 153.019, [ORS 153.020](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.020%20Presumptive%20fines%3B%20highway%20work%20zones%2C%20school%20zones%20and%20safety%20corridors.) and [ORS 153.021](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.021%20Minimum%20fines%3B%20waiver%3B%20audit%20of%20court.). If a specific fine statute does not name a presumptive amount, the presumptive fine is 20 percent of the maximum or the statutory minimum, whichever is greater (ORS 153.019).

Some counties also add a $5 courthouse surcharge, which becomes part of the presumptive fine ([ORS 1.188](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_1.188)).

## What do "presumptive," "minimum" and "maximum" fine mean?

The three numbers matter at different points in a case.

- **Presumptive fine.** This is the amount printed on the ticket ([ORS 153.051](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_153.051)). If you plead no contest and the court accepts the plea, the fine cannot be more than the presumptive fine ([ORS 153.090](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_153.090)). You may send a written explanation with a no contest plea, and the court may consider it.
- **Minimum fine.** The court generally cannot go below the minimum, even after a no contest plea with an explanation. A court may waive all or part of the minimum if requiring it would be "inconsistent with justice," looking at your finances and other obligations (ORS 153.021).
- **Maximum fine.** If the case goes to trial and you are found guilty, or a default judgment is entered because you did not appear, the court may impose any fine up to the statutory maximum (ORS 153.090).

There is one more wrinkle. If you plead no contest and the court decides the presumptive fine is too low because of prior convictions or the nature of the offense, it can require a trial unless you pay an additional amount, but never more than the maximum ([ORS 153.061](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.061%20Appearance%20by%20defendant.)).

## Which zones make an Oregon traffic fine higher?

The higher presumptive fines in the table apply only when the officer notes on the citation that the offense happened in one of these places (ORS 153.020):

- **Highway work zone.** An area marked by advance warning where road construction, repair or maintenance is being done, whether or not workers are actually present ([ORS 811.230](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.230)).
- **Posted school zone.** Signs must be posted, and either children must be present or a flashing light must be operating ([ORS 811.235](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.235)).
- **State safety corridor.** A highway segment the Oregon Department of Transportation has chosen and signed as a place where fines are doubled ([ORS 811.483](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.483)).
- **County safety corridor.** Under a law in effect since January 1, 2026, a county board of commissioners may designate up to two segments of county road at a time as safety corridors ([ORS 810.045](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors810.html); [House Bill 2154 (2025)](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2025R1/Measures/Overview/HB2154)).

In these zones, the higher fines apply to Class A and Class B traffic violations, and to Class C and Class D violations only when they involve speeding (ORS 811.230). Certain crimes committed in these zones, such as DUII, reckless driving and criminal driving while suspended, carry their own higher minimum fines.

## How does speed decide the class of a speeding ticket?

Speeding shows clearly how the class system works. Under [ORS 811.109](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.109):

| How far over the limit       | Class on most roads                                                             | Class where the limit is 65 mph or more |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| 1 to 10 mph                  | Class D                                                                         | Class C                                 |
| 11 to 20 mph                 | Class C                                                                         | Class B                                 |
| 21 to 30 mph                 | Class B                                                                         | Class A                                 |
| More than 30 mph             | Class A                                                                         | Class A                                 |
| 100 mph or more, on any road | Specific fine violation: presumptive fine $1,150 and a 30- to 90-day suspension | Same                                    |

For example, 75 mph in a 65 zone is a Class C violation, with a $165 presumptive fine. The same 10 mph over in a 35 zone is a Class D violation. A court may also suspend a license for up to 30 days when a driver is more than 30 mph over and has a prior speeding conviction within 12 months (ORS 811.109).

## What happens if you ignore an Oregon traffic ticket?

A ticket has a date by which you must make a "first appearance." You can do that by going to court, by asking for a trial in writing, or by pleading no contest and paying the presumptive fine (ORS 153.061). The Oregon Judicial Department's [traffic options page](https://www.courts.oregon.gov/services/online/Pages/Traffic-Options.aspx) explains each route and notes that online fine reductions are not available for some tickets, including speeds over 100 mph and careless driving.

If you do nothing:

- The court may enter a default judgment ([ORS 153.102](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_153.102)), and the fine can be anything up to the maximum (ORS 153.090).
- For a traffic violation, the court notifies DMV ([ORS 809.220](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.220)). DMV then sends notice that your driving privileges will be suspended in 60 days unless you show a court notice clearing it ([ORS 809.416](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.416)).
- Knowingly failing to appear on a violation citation is itself a crime, a Class A misdemeanor ([ORS 153.992](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_153.992)).

Driving after that kind of suspension creates a new problem. Our post on [whether driving while suspended is a crime in Oregon](/blog/driving-while-suspended-oregon-violation-or-crime/) explains when it is a violation and when it is a misdemeanor.

## Can a violation affect your license or a CDL?

Yes, even though it is not a crime. Oregon DMV's [suspensions page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/DMV/pages/driverid/suspreasons.aspx) says that for drivers 18 and older, three convictions or preventable accidents (or a mix) within 24 months lead to a 30-day restriction on driving between midnight and 5 am, and five lead to a 30-day suspension.

For commercial drivers, Oregon law says a court may not defer a judgment or allow a diversion that would keep a traffic conviction off a commercial driver's record (ORS 153.090). If you drive for a living, see our page on [CDL ticket defense in Oregon](/traffic-tickets/cdl-commercial-drivers/).

## How does a violation trial work?

A violation trial is in front of a judge, with no jury. The state must prove the violation by a preponderance of the evidence, and you cannot be required to testify. The court does not appoint a lawyer in a case where only violations are charged, and a district attorney generally does not appear unless the defense has a lawyer ([ORS 153.076](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_153.076)).

## What should you check before the date on your ticket?

- The class and presumptive fine printed on the ticket.
- Whether the officer marked a work zone, school zone or safety corridor.
- The first appearance date and the court named on the ticket.
- Whether you hold a commercial driver license.
- Your recent driving record, since prior convictions can affect the outcome and DMV action.
- Whether a no contest plea, a written explanation or a trial fits your situation.

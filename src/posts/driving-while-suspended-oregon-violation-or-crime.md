Is driving while suspended a crime in Oregon? Sometimes. Driving while suspended or revoked is usually a Class A traffic violation under [ORS 811.175](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.175), which means a fine but no jail. It becomes a crime under [ORS 811.182](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.182) only when the suspension came from one of the causes that statute lists, such as a DUII, an implied consent suspension, reckless driving (for driving before January 1, 2027) or a hit and run involving injury.

So the question that matters most is not "was I suspended?" but "why was I suspended?" This post walks through the three levels, the penalties for each, a change that takes effect on January 1, 2027, and the defenses the law allows.

## What is the difference between violation DWS and criminal DWS?

Oregon has two driving while suspended offenses (often shortened to DWS).

**Violation driving while suspended or revoked** (ORS 811.175) covers:

- Driving on a highway while a court or DMV has suspended or revoked your driving privileges, or your right to apply for them.
- Driving outside the limits of a hardship permit.
- Driving a commercial motor vehicle while your driving or commercial driving privileges are suspended or revoked in Oregon or any other jurisdiction.

It also applies on any premises open to the public, not just public roads. It is a Class A traffic violation "except as otherwise provided in ORS 811.182."

**Criminal driving while suspended or revoked** (ORS 811.182) is the same act, but the underlying suspension or revocation is one that the statute lists. Depending on the cause, it is a Class A misdemeanor or a Class B felony.

## Which suspensions make DWS a misdemeanor or a felony?

This table follows ORS 811.182 as it reads in the 2025 edition of the Oregon Revised Statutes.

| Why the license was suspended or revoked                                                                                                                     | What driving during it is                     | Where it is in ORS 811.182 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Murder, manslaughter, criminally negligent homicide or assault resulting from driving; aggravated vehicular homicide; aggravated DWS; felony DUII revocation | Class B felony                                | (3)                        |
| Misdemeanor DUII conviction                                                                                                                                  | Class A misdemeanor                           | (4)(k)                     |
| Implied consent suspension: refusing a breath or blood test, or a result of 0.08 or more (0.04 in a commercial vehicle, any amount under 21)                 | Class A misdemeanor                           | (4)(b)                     |
| Reckless driving                                                                                                                                             | Class A misdemeanor (changes January 1, 2027) | (4)(i)                     |
| Recklessly endangering, menacing or criminal mischief resulting from driving (criminal mischief changes January 1, 2027)                                     | Class A misdemeanor                           | (4)(a)                     |
| Hit and run involving injury (failure to perform the duties of a driver to injured persons)                                                                  | Class A misdemeanor                           | (4)(h)                     |
| Fleeing or attempting to elude police                                                                                                                        | Class A misdemeanor                           | (4)(j)                     |
| Habitual offender revocation                                                                                                                                 | Class A misdemeanor                           | (4)(f)                     |
| A felony with a vehicle element, or using a vehicle in a felony                                                                                              | Class A misdemeanor                           | (4)(g), (4)(L)             |
| Certain commercial license suspensions                                                                                                                       | Class A misdemeanor                           | (4)(c) to (4)(e)           |
| Any other suspension, such as one for failing to appear on a traffic ticket                                                                                  | Class A traffic violation                     | ORS 811.175                |

The last row is the one many people fall into. Oregon DMV notes that it may suspend driving privileges for failing to appear in an Oregon court on a ticket, and that suspension lasts until the court sends proof of clearance or 10 years pass ([Oregon DMV](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/DMV/pages/driverid/suspreasons.aspx)). That kind of suspension is not on the ORS 811.182 list, so driving during it is the violation, not the crime.

If your suspension came from a breath test refusal or failure, our post on [breath test refusal suspensions and hardship permits](/blog/refusing-breath-test-oregon-suspension-hardship-permit/) explains how long it lasts.

## What are the penalties for each level?

| Level                     | Jail or prison | Fine                             |
| ------------------------- | -------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Class A traffic violation | None           | Presumptive $440, maximum $2,000 |
| Class A misdemeanor       | Up to 364 days | Up to $6,250                     |
| Class B felony            | Up to 10 years | Up to $250,000                   |

Sources: [ORS 153.018](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.018%20Maximum%20fines.), [ORS 153.019](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html#:~:text=153.019%20Presumptive%20fines%3B%20generally.), [ORS 161.615](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.615), [ORS 161.635](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.635), [ORS 161.605](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.605), [ORS 161.625](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_161.625) and ORS 811.182. These are legal maximums, not predictions. One minimum also applies: if the underlying suspension came from DUII, the court must impose a fine of at least $1,000 for a first criminal DWS conviction and at least $2,000 for a second or later one (ORS 811.182). Our guide to [Oregon traffic violation classes and fines](/blog/oregon-traffic-violation-classes-fines/) explains how violation fines work.

Other consequences can follow, depending on the facts:

- **Impound at the stop.** An officer who has probable cause to believe you were driving while suspended, at either level, may have the vehicle impounded ([ORS 809.720](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.720)).
- **Impound after conviction.** A court may order a vehicle impounded or immobilized after a DWS conviction ([ORS 809.700](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.700)).
- **Forfeiture.** A vehicle may be seized for forfeiture if the driver is arrested or cited for criminal DWS and was convicted of criminal or aggravated DWS within the prior three years ([ORS 809.740](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.740)).
- **Habitual offender revocation.** Three convictions within five years from a list that includes criminal DWS, DUII and reckless driving lead to a five-year revocation ([ORS 809.600](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.600)).
- **Higher minimum fines in zones.** Criminal DWS in a highway work zone carries a minimum fine of 20 percent of the maximum ([ORS 811.230](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.230)). School zones and safety corridors have the same rule.
- **Aggravated DWS.** If a person knowingly drives while suspended for a criminal offense involving a vehicle and causes serious physical injury or death, it is aggravated driving while suspended, a Class C felony ([ORS 163.196](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_163.196)).

## What changes on January 1, 2027?

The 2026 Legislature passed [House Bill 4041](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2026R1/Measures/Overview/HB4041), signed March 5, 2026. It removes two causes from the misdemeanor list in ORS 811.182:

- A suspension for **reckless driving**.
- A suspension for **criminal mischief** resulting from driving. (Recklessly endangering and menacing stay on the list.)

The change becomes operative January 1, 2027, and applies only to conduct on or after that date ([HB 4041 enrolled text](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2026R1/Downloads/MeasureDocument/HB4041/Enrolled), section 2). Driving during one of those suspensions before January 1, 2027, is still judged under the current law. On or after that date, it falls back to the Class A traffic violation.

## What if you did not know your license was suspended?

Oregon law gives two affirmative defenses to both DWS offenses ([ORS 811.180](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.180)):

- **Emergency.** An injury or immediate threat of injury to a person or animal made it necessary to drive at that time and place.
- **No notice.** You had not received notice of the suspension or revocation, and a judge had not told you about it.

The no-notice defense is not available if you refused to accept DMV's notice, if the notice could not be delivered because you did not report a change of address to DMV, if a judge told you at a court appearance that your license was being suspended, if you actually knew about the suspension by any means, or if you received a notice of intent to suspend under the implied consent law. Whether either defense fits is a fact question for your lawyer.

## Does it matter if you were driving on a hardship permit?

Yes. Driving outside the limits of a hardship permit, such as outside the allowed hours or purpose, counts as driving while suspended (ORS 811.175). If the permit was issued during a suspension on the ORS 811.182 list, such as an implied consent or DUII suspension, the offense is criminal (ORS 811.182).

## What should you do right now?

- **Stop driving until your status is valid.** DMV's guidance is not to drive until you have reinstated and hold a valid license or permit ([Oregon DMV](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/DMV/pages/driverid/suspreasons.aspx)).
- **Check your status.** DMV explains how to check your driving privilege through your DMV2U account or by calling DMV Customer Assistance ([Oregon DMV fine remittance page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/DMV/Pages/Fine_Remittance.aspx)).
- **Find out why you were suspended.** That answer decides whether you face a violation or a crime.
- **Act fast on an impound.** A hearing request must be made within five calendar days after the impound notice is mailed, not counting weekends and holidays ([ORS 809.716](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_809.716)).
- **Keep every court date.** Missing one can add another suspension.
- **Tell your lawyer if you hold a CDL.** Commercial drivers face extra rules; see our page on [CDL ticket defense in Oregon](/traffic-tickets/cdl-commercial-drivers/).

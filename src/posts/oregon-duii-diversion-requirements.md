The DUII diversion requirements Oregon law sets fall into two groups: who can get in, and what you must do once you are in. To qualify, you generally cannot have another DUII charge pending, a DUII conviction or diversion in the past 15 years, a commercial driver license, or a crash that killed or injured someone else ([ORS 813.215](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.215)). Once in, you plead guilty or no contest, the court holds off on a conviction for one year, and you can ask for dismissal if you complete every condition ([ORS 813.230](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.230)).

DUII is Oregon's legal term for what most states call DUI: driving under the influence of intoxicants ([ORS 813.010](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.010)). This post covers eligibility, the petition, what the agreement requires and how it ends. It is general information. Whether diversion fits your case depends on the facts.

## What is DUII diversion?

The program in ORS 813.200 is the only form of diversion Oregon law allows for a DUII charge, and a DUII charge also cannot be pleaded down to a different offense in exchange for dismissal ([ORS 813.170](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.170)). At arraignment, the court must tell you that a diversion agreement may be available if you qualify and file a petition ([ORS 813.200](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.200)).

When the court allows the petition, it accepts your guilty or no contest plea but holds off on entering a conviction. The case is paused for the diversion period. The court notifies DMV, and the diversion agreement becomes part of your driving record ([ORS 813.230](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.230)).

## Who qualifies for DUII diversion in Oregon?

You must meet every condition in ORS 813.215. In plain terms:

| Requirement                                                                                                                                                      | Statute                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| No other DUII charge pending (in Oregon or elsewhere) on the day you file                                                                                        | ORS 813.215(1)(a)          |
| No DUII conviction from 15 years before this offense through the filing date                                                                                     | ORS 813.215(1)(b)          |
| Never convicted of felony DUII                                                                                                                                   | ORS 813.215(1)(c)          |
| Not in a DUII diversion or similar alcohol or drug rehabilitation program when you file, and none in the prior 15 years (with narrow exceptions)                 | ORS 813.215(1)(d)–(e), (3) |
| No pending charge, and no conviction in 15 years, for aggravated vehicular homicide, murder, manslaughter, criminally negligent homicide or assault from driving | ORS 813.215(1)(f)–(g)      |
| You did not hold commercial driving privileges on the date of the offense                                                                                        | ORS 813.215(1)(h)          |
| You were not driving a commercial motor vehicle                                                                                                                  | ORS 813.215(1)(i)          |
| The offense did not involve a crash that killed anyone or injured someone other than you                                                                         | ORS 813.215(1)(j)          |

A second or later diversion is possible only if you meet all of those conditions and have no conviction for any other criminal offense involving a motor vehicle in the same 15-year window ([ORS 813.215](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.215)).

Meeting the list does not mean the court must approve the petition. The court must consider whether diversion will benefit you and the community. It may also weigh whether you are likely to cooperate with treatment and follow the agreement, and whether a passenger under 18 was in the vehicle. The court must deny the petition if you failed to appear at arraignment without good cause ([ORS 813.220](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.220)).

## When and how do you file the petition?

The petition is filed with the court after the charge is filed. It must be filed within 30 days after your first appearance, unless the court allows a later date for good cause. Filing a demurrer, a motion to suppress or a motion for an omnibus hearing does not count as good cause. You cannot file after a guilty or no contest plea, or after trial begins ([ORS 813.210](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.210)).

There is one timing exception. If the charge alleges a controlled substance or inhalant and a blood or urine sample was taken, you may have until 14 days after the prosecutor sends you or your lawyer the lab results, if you had not been told your blood alcohol level or it was under 0.08.

You must serve a copy of the petition on the district attorney or city attorney, who has 15 days to file a written objection and ask for a hearing ([ORS 813.210](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.210)). If the DUII damaged someone else's property, that person has a right to be heard at any diversion hearing ([ORS 813.222](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.222)).

The Oregon Judicial Department's [DUII Diversion Petition and Agreement](https://www.courts.oregon.gov/forms/Documents/DUII_Diversion_Form_1_Petition_Agreement.pdf) notes that the Declaration of Eligibility and the Petition to Plead Guilty or No Contest must be filed with it. If the court grants the petition, you must be booked on the charge if you have not been already ([ORS 813.245](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.245)).

## What does the diversion agreement require?

The core terms come from ORS 813.200 and the sections around it. Use this as a checklist.

- **A plea.** Your petition includes a signed guilty or no contest plea ([ORS 813.200](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.200)). If the court denies the petition, that plea cannot be used against you in the case ([ORS 813.230](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.230)).
- **Fees.** A $490 filing fee to the court and $150 to the screening agency ([ORS 813.240](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.240)). The court may allow installments and may waive all or part of the filing fee for a defendant who cannot pay. You also agree to pay any court-appointed attorney fees and any restitution the court orders.
- **Screening and treatment.** You complete a screening interview at an agency the court designates, then any treatment the screening finds necessary, at your own expense based on ability to pay. Statements you make about the offense to program staff during screening or treatment cannot be used as evidence in a case arising from the same conduct ([ORS 813.250](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.250)).
- **No intoxicants.** You agree not to use intoxicants during diversion. The exceptions are sacramental wine in a religious service, a valid prescription taken as directed, and a nonprescription drug used as its label directs. The OJD form spells out that this includes marijuana.
- **Victim impact panel.** In counties that have a victim impact program, the court may require you to attend a session and pay a fee of $5 to $50 ([ORS 813.235](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.235)).
- **Ignition interlock.** The court must require an approved interlock in any vehicle you drive during diversion, while you have driving privileges, if a test showed 0.08 percent or more, if you refused a test, or if a test showed some alcohol under 0.08 plus another intoxicant. It may require one in other cases. A medical exemption is available under DMV rules ([ORS 813.602](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.602)). After at least six consecutive months with no negative device reports, and while in compliance with any required treatment, you can ask the court to lift the interlock requirement ([ORS 813.645](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.645)).
- **No new DUII or open container violation.** The petition warns that either one during the diversion period violates the agreement ([ORS 811.170](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_811.170)).
- **A current address.** You must keep the court advised of your mailing address.
- **Former jeopardy waiver.** You give up former jeopardy rights for later prosecution on the charge or other offenses from the same episode. The OJD form adds that diversion covers only the DUII charge; other charges from the same incident are prosecuted separately.

> **Note:** Oregon's definition of "intoxicant" is broad. It covers alcohol, cannabis, controlled substances, inhalants, psilocybin and other impairing drugs. Our post on [DUI vs DUII in Oregon](/blog/dui-vs-duii-oregon/) explains the definition.

## How long does diversion last, and can it be extended?

A DUII diversion agreement lasts one year from the date the court allows the petition ([ORS 813.230](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.230)).

You can ask for more time by motion filed within the last 30 days of the diversion period. The court may grant it if you made a good faith effort and can finish the remaining conditions within the extension. An extension can be no more than 180 days, and generally only one is allowed. Members of the armed forces, reserves or National Guard on active duty have broader options ([ORS 813.225](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.225)). If the court denies the extension, it enters the plea, enters a conviction and sentences you.

## How does diversion end?

| Situation                                                  | What happens                                                                                                                                                   | Statute     |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| You completed every condition                              | After the period ends, you can move to dismiss the charge with prejudice. If you do not file within six months, the court may dismiss on its own motion if it finds you complied. | ORS 813.250 |
| Everything is done except $500 or less in fees             | Within 180 days after the period ends, you can move for dismissal; the court dismisses if you pay the balance by 5 pm on the hearing day.                      | [ORS 813.252](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.252) |
| The court believes you violated terms or no longer qualify | The court issues an order to show cause. If you do not appear, or the court finds a violation, it terminates diversion, enters the plea and sentences you.     | ORS 813.255 |

If diversion is terminated, the court may take into account any partial completion at sentencing. It may not terminate diversion only because restitution is unpaid if you met every other condition ([ORS 813.255](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.255)). The OJD form also says that if you do not receive a notice of dismissal at the end of the year, you must file a motion asking for it.

## Does diversion stop the DMV suspension?

No. Diversion resolves the criminal charge, and the implied consent suspension is a separate DMV action. The information officers give at the time of the test says the outcome of a criminal DUII charge will not affect the suspension ([ORS 813.130](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.130)). That is why the 10-day hearing deadline in our post on [what to do after a DUI arrest in Oregon](/blog/what-to-do-after-duii-arrest-oregon/) still matters if you plan to seek diversion.

Diversion also counts later. Being in diversion, or having started one in the past five years, lengthens a future implied consent suspension ([ORS 813.430](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_813.430)). Having taken part in diversion within 15 years also blocks another diversion under ORS 813.215. Our [Oregon DUII diversion](/duii-defense/diversion/) page covers how these pieces fit together. Our post on [whether to request an implied consent hearing](/blog/should-you-request-implied-consent-hearing/) covers the DMV side.
